Part B: Product Analysis

Primary Function

The product I chose converts simple movements of the hand into lever force that is used to cut and separate materials. Converting the movement into lever action gives the user a mechanical advantage, allowing the material to be cut more easily.

Governing Model

The main model used to describe the behavior of the scissors is the moment equation, M = Fd, where M is the moment, F is the force applied by the fingers, and d is the distance from the point where the force is applied to the pivot. The scissors function as a lever, with the pivot acting as the fulcrum. The resulting rotation causes the two blades to move past one another, producing the shearing action used to separate or cut the material.

Assumption

The blades and handles are assumed to behave as rigid members, and the pivot is treated as a fixed fulcrum. This allows deformation of the components to be neglected when analyzing the primary lever behavior.

Part C: Component Geometry

The image above identifies two important geometric features of the scissors: the pivot, circled at the top, and the blade, circled at the bottom. The pivot is located between the handle and blades. When force is applied, the pivot transfers the force from the handle into the blades, allowing them to cross one another. The blade uses this motion and converts it into a shearing action at the edge, allowing it to cut material. These features work together to convert the user's input force into the shearing motion required to separate material.

Pivot

The pivot is located between the handles and blades and acts as the fulcrum of the lever system. Its position determines the relative lengths of the handle and blade lever arms. When force is applied to the handles, the pivot constrains the members to rotate about this point, producing the motion required for the blades to pass across one another.

Blade

The blade transfers the rotational motion produced around the pivot into a shearing action at the cutting edge. The tapered geometry of the blade concentrates the interaction with the material near the cutting edge, allowing the material to be separated as the two blades move past one another.

Part D: Patent Research

Patent

The patent is US20110016728A1 – Ergonomic Scissors , developed by Abbe J. Hann and Ronald B. Hann. The patent describes scissors as two cutting members connected by a pivot.

Alternative Solutions

Two alternative products that perform the same primary function are a knife and a rotary cutter. A knife uses a single cutting edge to separate material through a sliding motion, while a rotary cutter uses a circular blade that rolls across the material. Both cut material but use different mechanisms from the pivoting and shearing action of scissors.

Design Decision

One design decision I identified is the placement of the pivot between the handles and blades. This allows the scissors to function as a lever, so force applied farther from the pivot at the handles produces a moment that rotates the blades. Positioning the user's fingers farther from the pivot increases the moment produced for a given hand force, allowing more cutting force to be produced at the blades.
